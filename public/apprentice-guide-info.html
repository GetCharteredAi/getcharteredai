The Chartered Surveyor
Apprenticeship Route

Earn a salary, gain real experience and work toward MRICS — all at the same time. Everything you need to know before you apply.

What the apprenticeship route actually is
💷
Earn from day one
You are employed from the start. Paid a salary throughout. No waiting until graduation to begin your career.
🎓
RICS-accredited degree
You study part-time — typically one day a week or block release — for a RICS-accredited degree alongside your job.
🏆
Leads toward MRICS
The route is structured toward Chartered Surveyor status. Strong apprentices can reach MRICS in around five years with no tuition debt.
The four stages of the journey
01
Secure your apprenticeship
Apply to firms from September to March for the following summer or autumn start. Most require 3 A-levels and GCSE Maths and English as a minimum. Some firms accept career changers with relevant work experience.
02
Degree + workplace experience
Five years for most routes. You work Monday to Thursday, study on Friday or in blocks. Structured competency development throughout — this is where you build your APC evidence as you go.
03
End-point assessment
Near the end of the programme you complete a professional assessment. This typically involves a case study, portfolio of competency evidence, professional discussion and demonstration of ethical standards.
04
Chartered status — MRICS
Once all requirements are successfully met you can become a Chartered Surveyor. This is not automatic — it requires the right experience, evidence and readiness at the required standard.

What employers are actually looking for
✓
Genuine interest in property, construction or land — not just "a good career"
✓
Commercial awareness — basic understanding of how property and business interact
✓
Confidence communicating with people — clients, colleagues and contractors
✓
Problem-solving mindset — can you think through a challenge methodically?
✓
Professional attitude — punctual, reliable, willing to learn from senior colleagues
→
Do not only target CBRE and JLL. Regional firms, councils and housing associations often have fewer applicants and excellent training.

Salary usually rises each year as you progress through the programme. By the time you reach chartered status, salaries of £35,000–£55,000+ are typical depending on pathway and location.
No tuition fees means take-home pay is significantly better than a full-time student's position.

✓
No student debt — financial position significantly stronger from the start

Employer network and professional relationships built over five years — not starting from scratch

Competency evidence built continuously — not assembled in the final months

What to think carefully about before applying
→
Balancing full-time work, university deadlines and professional diary records simultaneously is demanding
→
Chartered status is not automatic at year five — you need the right experience, evidence and readiness
→
If you dislike deadlines, juggling priorities or regular client-facing work — think carefully
→
The profession is broader than most people realise — surveyors work in sustainability, data, AI property roles, rural estates, stadiums and infrastructure, not just offices
