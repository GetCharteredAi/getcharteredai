The challenge

What happens after onboarding?

Once induction is complete and real work begins, developing professionals are no longer just learning systems and tasks. They are learning when to ask, when to act, how to explain their reasoning and how to exercise judgement — and that does not happen automatically.

The developing professional…

Am I doing enough?
When should I ask for help?
Am I progressing as expected?

The manager…

Where are they doing well?
Where do they need more experience?
What should we focus on next?

Professional Readiness brings both perspectives together, creating a clearer picture of development and what should happen next.
One product. Two development points.

Entering a professional role. Progressing within it.

Professional Readiness supports two different moments in development — one focused on the transition into professional work, the other on progression as experience and responsibility grow.

What makes it different

The right support starts with understanding the need.

Two people may appear to be struggling in the same area, but for very different reasons.

Person A

Needs more practice

They receive regular feedback but aren’t yet putting it into practice.

Next step: Targeted practice and follow-up.

Person B

Needs more experience

They understand what’s expected but haven’t had the opportunity to demonstrate it.

Next step: Relevant workplace experience and feedback.

Professional Readiness helps identify what is holding someone back, so managers can focus on the support that will make a difference.

Development Priority · Workplace Exposure

The next step is workplace experience.

She understands how to respond to a client challenge and communicates well internally. What she hasn’t yet had is enough direct client-facing experience to put that understanding into practice.

Suggested Next Step

Give her the opportunity to take part in client conversations, with support and feedback from a more experienced colleague.

Across a cohort, appropriately aggregated development insights can help employers recognise recurring strengths, potential development gaps and areas where further workplace experience or support may be useful.
