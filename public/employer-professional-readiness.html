The starting point

What happens once real work begins?

Once onboarding is complete, developing professionals are no longer just learning systems and tasks. They are learning how to operate professionally — when to ask, act, escalate, explain their reasoning and exercise judgement. That does not happen automatically.

The developing professional may be thinking…

Am I doing enough?

When should I ask for help?

How much initiative should I take?

What does “good” look like at this stage?

Am I progressing as expected?

The manager may be asking…

Where are they progressing well?

Where do they need more exposure?

Are they beginning to exercise appropriate judgement?

What should we focus on next?

Are expectations clear enough?

Professional Readiness starts here — helping make early development clearer for both the individual and the manager, and giving both sides something structured to build from.

What makes the diagnosis useful

Not just where development is needed — why.

Two people can appear to be developing in the same area for completely different reasons. Knowing the cause changes the response.

Person A — Application

Has the opportunity, but is not yet converting feedback into changed practice.

Regular feedback conversations are happening. The issue is turning feedback into deliberate professional adjustment.

What helps next: more deliberate feedback-to-action practice.

Person B — Exposure

Understands the principle, but has not had enough workplace opportunity to demonstrate it.

The understanding is there. The missing ingredient is structured opportunity and feedback exposure.

What helps next: greater workplace opportunity and structured feedback conversations.

Stage 02  ·  Practise & Apply

Somewhere safe to apply what you know before it counts.

Knowing something and being able to apply it under questioning are different things. The gap between formal learning and confident workplace performance is where most development stalls.

Formal learning → Safe practice → Workplace application
Get Chartered AI provides the practice layer in the middle — so development does not stop at the point of learning.

Michael — the practice and challenge layer

Michael gives the individual a safe place to apply knowledge, reason through problems and respond to challenge — without the pressure of a live workplace situation. The goal is not simply to score performance but to support improvement through practice, retry and reflection.

Applies knowledge

Works through real professional situations, not abstract questions.

Responds to follow-up

Follows up on answers to probe reasoning rather than accepting surface responses.

Explains and defends decisions

Practises articulating why — not just what.

Challenges reasoning

Builds professional judgement

Practice then goes back into the workplace. The aim is not to replace real experience — it is to make the most of it when it happens.

Stage 03  ·  Evidence

Development should be visible in the work itself.

Training completed is not the same as capability developed. The employer should be able to ask a concrete question about each development priority:

What would demonstrate that this capability has actually developed?

What workplace evidence might look like

  • Taking on a more complex task
  • Demonstrating better judgement in a real situation
  • Handling a client conversation independently
  • Recognising when to escalate — and doing it
  • Explaining reasoning more clearly to a manager
  • Applying knowledge without being prompted

The important distinction

Formal learning creates awareness. Practice builds the capability to apply it. But it is in real work, with real consequences, that capability is properly developed and demonstrated.

The employer’s role is to create opportunities for that demonstration to happen — and to notice when it does.

Evidence should come from real work where possible. The aim is to connect development activity to what happens in the workplace, not to create an additional assessment layer.
Stage 04  ·  Review

Has anything actually changed?

The later review is not a separate product — it is the same connected approach used again, asking the question that makes the whole system meaningful.

What the later review establishes

What has improved

Where development priorities have moved. What is now being demonstrated consistently that was not before.

Where development is still needed

What has not yet moved. Where experience, practice or exposure has been insufficient. What was a priority then, and whether it still is.

What the next priority should be

As capability develops, what matters most will change. The later review surfaces the next development priority, not just the last.

Deeper technical review. The later review can also include pathway-specific technical questions and professional judgement scenarios — showing whether learning and workplace experience are translating into technical capability, not just professional awareness.

Training need or opportunity need?
If a group of developing professionals appears weaker in client care, the useful question is why. Is it a knowledge or practice issue — or have people simply not had enough client-facing exposure?

Those require different responses. And the same system that identified the individual gap provides the signal for the organisational picture.

Workforce Intelligence does not make promotion or employment decisions. It provides a more structured basis for the conversations and decisions that managers and organisations already make.
