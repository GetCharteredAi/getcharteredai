Professional Readiness

From development need to demonstrated capability.

A connected approach that helps employers identify where development is needed, give people somewhere to practise and apply what they know, connect that development to workplace evidence, and review whether capability has actually changed.

Thoughtfully designed. Human-led. AI-assisted.

The starting point

What happens once real work begins?

Once onboarding is complete, developing professionals are no longer just learning systems and tasks. They are learning how to operate professionally — when to ask, act, escalate, explain their reasoning and exercise judgement. That does not happen automatically.

The developing professional may be thinking…

Am I doing enough?

When should I ask for help?

How much initiative should I take?

What does “good” look like at this stage?

Am I progressing as expected?

The manager may be asking…

Where are they progressing well?

Where do they need more exposure?

Are they beginning to exercise appropriate judgement?

What should we focus on next?

Are expectations clear enough?

One connected development system

Identify → Practise & Apply → Evidence → Review

Professional Readiness is one connected programme — not a collection of separate products. It helps people identify what needs development, practise applying what they know, demonstrate it in real work and return later to see what has changed.

Identify

Surface strengths, development priorities and whether the next need is knowledge, practice, experience, exposure or confidence.

Practise & Apply

A safe place to explain reasoning, respond to challenge and strengthen judgement before taking that practice back into the workplace.

Evidence

Development is demonstrated through what the individual can actually do in real work — not simply through training attendance.

Review

Return later to see what has developed, what still needs attention and what should happen next.

For managers

Something useful to have a conversation about.

Get Chartered AI structures the evidence and conversation so managers can focus their judgement where it matters most. The system does not replace the manager’s role — it makes the conversation more useful before it begins.

Strengths and development priorities

A clearer view of what is already being demonstrated and what would make the greatest difference next.

Useful development prompts

Practical conversation starters, opportunities and actions matched to the individual.

Workplace evidence ideas

A clearer sense of what real activity would demonstrate that development has moved.

Later review signals

A consistent way to see whether priorities have changed over time.

Employee privacy. Detailed employee responses remain private. Managers receive development outcomes, prompts and agreed actions — not a transcript of private reasoning.

A clearer starting point for development conversations.

Bring together the individual’s view, manager observation and workplace evidence before the conversation begins. This helps identify whether someone needs more knowledge, practice, experience, exposure or opportunity — and agree the right next step.

Workforce Intelligence

A consistent view across teams and intakes.

When the same approach is used consistently, individual development signals reveal patterns that are difficult to see one conversation at a time — including common strengths, recurring gaps and shortages in workplace opportunity or exposure.

Learning & Practice Workplace Exposure Recognition & Articulation Judgement & Responsibility Confidence Calibration
Training need or opportunity need?
If a group appears weaker in client care, the useful question is why. Is it a knowledge or practice issue — or have people simply not had enough client-facing exposure?

Those require different responses. And the same system that identified the individual gap provides the signal for the organisational picture.
As people progress

From early readiness to technical depth.

As people take on greater responsibility, Professional Readiness can deepen into pathway-specific technical review, professional-judgement scenarios and structured APC support. Chartership is one important stage in the wider journey — not a separate proposition.
