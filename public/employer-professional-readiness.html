The space after onboarding

So what happens after onboarding?

Once induction is complete and real work begins, developing professionals are no longer just learning systems and tasks. They are learning how to operate professionally — when to ask, act, escalate, explain their reasoning and exercise judgement.

The developing professional may be thinking…

Am I doing enough?

When should I ask for help?

How much initiative should I take?

What does “good” look like at this stage?

Am I progressing as expected?

The manager may be asking…

Where are they progressing well?

Where do they need more exposure?

Are they beginning to exercise appropriate judgement?

What should we focus on next?

Are expectations clear enough?

That is where Professional Readiness starts — helping make early development clearer for both the individual and the manager.

One product. Two development points.

Entering a professional role. Progressing within it.

Professional Readiness supports two different moments in development — one focused on the transition into professional work, the other on progression as experience and responsibility grow.

What makes the diagnosis different

Not just where development is needed — why.

Two people can appear to be developing in the same area for completely different reasons.

Person A — Application

Has the opportunity, but is not yet converting feedback into changed practice.

Regular feedback conversations are happening. The issue is turning feedback into deliberate professional adjustment.

What helps next: more deliberate feedback-to-action practice.

Person B — Exposure

Understands the principle, but has not had enough workplace opportunity to demonstrate it.

The understanding is there. The missing ingredient is structured opportunity and feedback exposure.

What helps next: greater workplace opportunity and structured feedback conversations.

Same apparent outcome. Different cause. Different action.

We don’t just identify where someone needs development — we help identify why, so the next action is more useful.
