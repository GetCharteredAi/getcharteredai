A closer look at Professional Readiness

What happens in the first few months after onboarding?

Professional Readiness supports that early transition — once onboarding is complete and real workplace development begins.

Once real work begins, developing professionals are no longer just learning tasks and systems. They are learning when to ask, when to act, how to explain their reasoning and how to exercise judgement — and that does not happen automatically.

For managers and counsellors

Better-prepared people. Better use of your time.

Structured AI support means managers and counsellors can spend their time where experience and judgement add most value — not doing the diagnostic work themselves.

A clearer development picture before the conversation

Professional Readiness brings together the employee’s own view and the manager’s observed perspective before a development conversation begins — so both sides arrive with a shared, structured picture rather than starting from scratch.

Development outcomes and conversation prompts

Managers receive relevant individual development outcomes and structured conversation starters — not raw private answers. The employee’s reasoning remains theirs.

Earlier visibility of development needs

Identify gaps in knowledge, application or exposure before they affect performance or assessment outcomes — rather than discovering them close to a submission or assessment date.

Structured APC support without replacing counsellors

Get Chartered AI is designed to sit alongside the people already supporting development. Candidates arrive at counsellor conversations better prepared and with clearer questions.

For the wider business

Workforce Intelligence

When the same structured approach is used across teams, those individual signals begin to build a wider picture.

When the same structured Professional Readiness approach is used consistently across teams and early-career groups, individual development signals can be examined collectively — making it easier to see recurring strengths, exposure needs, capability priorities and differences between teams or disciplines.
