Graduate Surveying —
First Interview Prep

What surveying firms are actually looking for. Built by Chartered Surveyors.

🎯

You have a first interview. Here is how to make it count.

First interviews in surveying are less about technical knowledge and more about who you are, why you want this career and whether you have done your homework. The firms that attract the best graduates ask the same core questions every time. This guide covers all of them.

01
Why surveying?
Motivation
What they want to hear
A genuine, specific answer — not "I like buildings." Connect it to something real: a placement, a project, a person whose career path inspired you, or a specific area of property that genuinely interests you. Have two reasons, not one.
Common mistake
Vague answers: "I always liked buildings." No specificity, no conviction. Interviewers hear this dozens of times a day.
02
Why this firm?
Research
What they want to hear
Something specific about the firm that you cannot say about every firm. Their market focus, a recent project or deal they were involved in, their APC support structure, their culture or values. Show you have spent time on their website and read their recent news.
Common mistake
"You are one of the largest firms in the UK." Every candidate says this. It tells them nothing about why you chose them specifically.
03
Tell me about yourself
Opening
What they want to hear
Two minutes maximum. Present → Past → Future. Start with who you are and what you have studied. Mention one or two relevant experiences. End with where you want to go and why this role is the next step. Practise it until it sounds natural, not rehearsed.
Common mistake
Reciting your CV chronologically. They have already read it. Give them context and direction — not a list of dates.
04
Commercial awareness
Knowledge
What they want to hear
Your view on something happening in the property market right now — interest rates, planning reform, sustainability requirements, rental market changes. You do not need all the answers. You need to show you are curious, informed and thinking about the industry you are joining.
Common mistake
Saying nothing. "I don't really follow the market yet" is the answer that ends interviews early.
05
Strengths and weaknesses
Self-awareness
What they want to hear
Real answers — not "I work too hard." For strengths, give one with evidence. For weaknesses, give one that is genuine, explain what you have done about it and show progress. Self-awareness is a professional skill. Show you have it.
Common mistake
Disguised strengths as weaknesses: "I am a perfectionist." Interviewers find this patronising. It shows a lack of self-awareness — the opposite of what they are testing for.
06
Competency questions
STAR method
What they want to hear
Specific examples structured with STAR — see below. Teamwork, problem-solving, communication and handling pressure are the four most common themes. Prepare one strong example for each. University projects, placements, part-time work and sport all count.
Common mistake
Answering in theory: "I would do X." They want to hear what you actually did. If you cannot give a real example, the answer does not register.
07
Three things to research
Preparation
Do this before every interview
1. The firm — their website, recent news, key clients and markets.
2. The role — what the graduate programme involves, which pathways they support, how they structure APC supervision.
3. The market — one or two current issues in property relevant to the firm's specialism.
Common mistake
Researching only the firm's homepage. Read their latest press releases, LinkedIn posts and any published deals or projects from the past six months.
08
Questions to ask them
Close strong
What they want to hear
Always ask something. Good questions: "How do you support graduates through the APC process?" "What does the first six months look like in this role?" "What do your most successful graduates have in common?" These show curiosity, professionalism and forward thinking.
Common mistake
"No, I think you have covered everything." This is the weakest possible close. It signals low engagement. Always have two questions ready.

The STAR Method — Use This for Every Competency Question

SSituation

Set the scene briefly. Where were you, what was the context?

TTask

What was your specific responsibility or challenge?

AAction

What did you personally do? Be specific — use "I" not "we".

RResult

What was the outcome? Quantify it if you can.
