The same problem can need a different answer

Not every development gap needs more training.

Someone can look hesitant for very different reasons. The useful question is: what is actually holding them back?

01

Knowledge

They genuinely need to learn or refresh something.

→ targeted learning
02

Practice

They know it, but need more chances to apply it.

→ structured practice
03

Experience

They need to do more of the work themselves.

→ ownership and repetition
04

Exposure

They simply have not encountered the situation yet.

→ access and observation
05

Articulation

They understand it, but struggle to explain their thinking.

→ questioning and spoken practice
06

Evidence recognition

They may already be demonstrating capability without recognising it.

→ reflection and evidence mapping
And sometimes there isn’t a gap at all. They’re ready for more scope.

One issue. Two perspectives.

A better development conversation starts with both sides.

Employee perspective

When you are unsure whether a decision sits within your responsibility, how do you decide whether to act or ask for guidance?

“I deal with routine tasks myself, but if it could affect cost or the client I usually check with my manager first.”
Michael might askWhat information would you need before deciding yourself? What would make escalation necessary?
Manager perspective

How does this person currently handle decisions at the edge of their responsibility?

“They recognise when they need guidance and do not take unnecessary risks, but they can still be cautious with decisions they are capable of making themselves.”
Useful differenceThe employee sees appropriate caution. The manager sees room for more ownership. That gives them something specific to discuss.
Not because one is right and one is wrong. Because the difference helps decide what should happen next.

Ready for the task — or ready for the role?

Doing the task is only part of professional readiness.

As people grow, they need to understand how their work fits into the wider project, team, client and business.

The taskWhat am I being asked to do?
The projectWhat does this affect — cost, programme, risk, quality?
The teamWho depends on this decision? Who needs to know?
The businessHow does my role contribute to wider commercial and organisational goals?
The professionWhat standards, judgement, accountability and limits apply?
Knowing what to do matters. Understanding why it matters is what helps someone take greater responsibility.

Defend · own · progress

The same model can continue beyond early career as the standard changes.

What “ready” means changes

Learn the work → Take responsibility for the work → Lead the work.

Starters

Learn the work.

Understand the task, contribute to the team, build knowledge, know when to ask and begin to see how the work fits the wider project.

Minders

Take responsibility for the work.

Apply judgement, make decisions, own outcomes, understand wider consequences and explain the reasoning behind what you do.

Leaders

Lead the work.

Guide others, shape decisions, balance business needs, manage risk, develop people and carry greater accountability.

The definition of “ready” changes as responsibility changes. The system changes with it.
