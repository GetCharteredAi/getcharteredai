Professional Readiness — guided example
1 · Employee experience

How am I actually developing?

The employee reflects privately on real workplace experience. The aim is not simply to ask whether they know something, but whether they can recognise it, apply it and explain their judgement.

Example area: Client care

A client challenges part of your advice. What do you do next?

Employee response “I would make sure I understood the concern, check the information I had relied on, explain my reasoning clearly and ask for support if the issue went beyond my experience or authority.”
Follow-up reflection “I understand what I should do, but I have had very little direct client-facing responsibility so far.”
What this starts to reveal
The employee may understand the right professional response — but still need more opportunity to demonstrate it in practice.
2 · Manager perspective

What is the manager seeing?

The manager adds a practical workplace view. This does not expose the employee’s private answers. It adds observed evidence and context.

Same development area: Client care
Manager observation

“She communicates clearly internally and responds well to feedback. She has not yet had much direct responsibility for client conversations because those have mainly been handled by senior team members.”

What appears strong Professional communication, willingness to ask for help, understanding of expected behaviour.
What may be limiting progress Limited workplace opportunity rather than an obvious knowledge problem.
Why both perspectives matter
Without the employee and manager context together, a weaker outcome could easily be treated as “needs more training”.
3 · Michael synthesis

Michael helps identify the cause — not just the outcome.

Michael brings the two perspectives together, spots where they align or differ, and turns that into clear points to discuss.

Employee signal

Understands the professional response and can explain the reasoning.

→
Manager signal

Confirms limited opportunity to demonstrate the behaviour directly with clients.

Likely development type: Workplace Exposure

The immediate priority is not another generic client-care course. The stronger next step is structured client-facing opportunity, with appropriate supervision and feedback.

Same apparent outcome. Different cause. Different action.

A person with regular client exposure but poor follow-through on feedback may need more practice and application. This person appears to need opportunity.
4 · Manager output

A better-prepared development conversation.

The manager does not receive the employee’s raw private responses. They receive a clear development picture and useful next-step prompts.

Strengths

Clear communication, professional awareness, appropriate escalation, responsiveness to feedback.

Development priority

Build confidence and judgement through more direct client-facing experience.

Suggested next action

Give structured client exposure with a senior colleague, followed by short reflective feedback.

For the manager
Less guesswork. A clearer reason for the development need. A more useful conversation about what should happen next.

And across teams?

When the same structured approach is used consistently, these individual signals can begin to reveal wider patterns — helping the business distinguish a learning need from an opportunity need.
