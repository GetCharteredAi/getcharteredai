The honest truth about referrals

The RICS referral letter names the competencies where the panel had concerns. What it does not tell you is whether the problem was in the written submission, the interview or both — and whether the issue was one of structure, evidence or content. Most referred candidates had a structural evidencing problem: they described their work rather than evidenced their professional judgement. Adding more words to an answer that already fails for that reason will not fix it. This programme starts there.

The programme

Nine modules. Structured to fix

Every module addresses a specific structural reason candidates are referred — not general revision, but targeted reconstruction of how you demonstrate professional competence in writing and under questioning.

Module CR01
Understanding Your Referral Properly

How to read the referral report calmly and accurately. Identifying patterns in assessor feedback. Separating technical gaps from performance gaps — and recognising whether the problem was in the written submission, the interview, or both.

Module CR02
Should I Keep My Case Study?

A structured assessment of whether your original case study gives you a stronger platform for the resit than starting with a new project. Criteria for making the decision clearly and professionally, not emotionally.

Module CR03
What APC Assessors Actually Score

Resetting your understanding of Level 1, Level 2 and Level 3 expectations. The difference between knowledge, application and decision-making. What professional judgement looks like in practice — and the 'safe pair of hands' standard you are being measured against.

Module CR04
Defending What You Submit

How to structure answers so they hold up under direct follow-up questioning. The patterns that cause a strong written submission to fall apart under the panel's probing — and how to rebuild your responses so they don't.

Module CR05
Evidence, Structure and Common Referral Patterns

The most common structural reasons candidates are referred — over-description, weak reasoning, generic answers, overuse of "we", shallow examples, poor Level 2 evidencing. How to identify these in your own answers and rebuild them with professional ownership.

Module CR06
Rebuilding Your Competency Evidence and Answers

All 11 mandatory competencies rebuilt with proper Level 2 evidencing. Updated for 2026 — ECCTA, BSR independence, RICS AI Standard, Renters Rights Act. The competencies most commonly flagged in referral letters addressed specifically.

Module CR07
Your Pathway Module

Deep-dive technical content for your specific RICS pathway — pathway-specific questions, current legislation and the technical frameworks assessors expect at Chartered level. Verified against the current RICS pathway guide.

Module CR08
Performing Under Pressure — Mock Practice and Your Resit Prep

Handling silence and pressure more effectively. Michael in Assessor Mode available 24 hours a day for repeat mock questioning on your weakest competencies. Plus a structured resit preparation plan: what to prioritise, how to build a practice routine and how to enter the room with a different mindset from the first attempt.

Module CR09
Reflection, Self-Awareness and Resit Mindset

Reflecting honestly on what went wrong. Understanding how confidence, clarity and professional judgement are connected. Rebuilding your answers with more maturity and insight — and leaving with a specific preparation plan so you are not guessing what to do next.
