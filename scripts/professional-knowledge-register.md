| Source review status | AUTHORITATIVE_VERIFIED |

### Verified professional requirements

The five current Rules of Conduct (effective 2 February 2022):

1. Members and firms must be honest, act with integrity and comply with their professional obligations, including obligations to RICS.
2. Members and firms must maintain their professional competence and ensure that services are provided by competent individuals who have the necessary expertise.
3. Members and firms must provide good-quality and diligent service.
4. Members and firms must treat others with respect and encourage diversity and inclusion.
5. Members and firms must act in the public interest, take responsibility for their actions and act to prevent harm and maintain public confidence in the profession.

APC candidates assessed from 2 February 2022 onwards are assessed on these Rules of Conduct.
The Ethics, Rules of Conduct and Professionalism competency is mandatory to Level 3 for all Chartered assessments.

### Historical framework (superseded)

The five Global Professional and Ethical Standards were superseded on 2 February 2022. They were:
1. Act with integrity
2. Always provide a high standard of service
3. Act in a way that promotes trust in the profession
4. Treat others with respect
5. Take responsibility

If a candidate specifically asks about these, explain they were superseded in February 2022 and give the current Rules of Conduct.

### Important qualifications

- Candidates who submitted before 2 February 2022 may have been assessed on the old standards; this applies only to historical submissions, not current training
- The underlying ethical principles are similar between old and new frameworks; the structure and wording changed
- The Rules of Conduct replaced three separate documents: Rules of Conduct for Members, Rules of Conduct for Firms, and the Global Professional and Ethical Standards

### Common errors to avoid

- Giving the seven mandatory APC competencies in response to a question about the RICS ethical framework — these are separate requirements
- Giving the old five Global Professional and Ethical Standards as the current framework
- Conflating the five Rules of Conduct with the five excepted purposes under Red Book PS 1 (which are different)

## PKR-02: Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 — Scope and Payment Provisions

| Field | Value |

| Applicable version | As amended by Local Democracy, Economic Development and Construction Act 2009 |
| Effective date | LDEDCA 2009 amendments commenced in England and Wales 1 October 2011 |
| Verification date | 24 September 2026 |
| Source consulted | legislation.gov.uk — s.104, s.105, s.106, s.110A, s.110B (HGCRA 1996) |
| Source review status | AUTHORITATIVE_VERIFIED (ss.104, 105, 106, 110A, 110B confirmed from legislation.gov.uk — 24 September 2026; Scheme for Construction Contracts Regulations not separately checked in this review) |

### Verified professional requirements

**Scope — what is a construction contract (s.104):**
A construction contract is an agreement for: (a) the carrying out of construction operations; (b) arranging for the carrying out of construction operations by others; (c) providing labour for construction operations.

s.104(2) explicitly states that the definition of construction contract INCLUDES an agreement to do architectural, design, or surveying work, or to provide advice on building, engineering, interior or exterior decoration or on the laying-out of landscape, in relation to construction operations.

s.104(3): contracts of employment are excluded.

**No monetary threshold:** There is no general monetary threshold in HGCRA 1996. The Act applies to qualifying construction contracts regardless of contract value.

**Residential occupier exclusion (s.106):** The Act does not apply to contracts with residential occupiers — contracts that principally relate to operations on a dwelling that one party occupies or intends to occupy as their residence. No monetary threshold is specified in s.106.

**Payment framework:** The Act requires construction contracts to provide for stage payments, payment notices, and pay less notices. Where a contract does not provide an adequate mechanism, the Scheme for Construction Contracts (England and Wales) Regulations 1998 as amended implies statutory terms.

**Stage payments — s.109:** A party is entitled to payment by instalments, stage payments or other periodic payments unless the contract specifies or the parties agree that the estimated work duration is less than 45 days. Amounts and intervals are for the parties to agree; the Scheme for Construction Contracts applies in the absence of adequate contractual provision.

**Payment notice — ss.110A and 110B:**

*Contractual requirement (s.110A):* Under s.110A(1), every construction contract must, in relation to each payment provided for by the contract, include one of the following:

(a) a requirement for the payer or a specified person to give a notice to the payee, specifying the sum considered to be due and the basis for calculation, no later than **5 days after the payment due date** (s.110A(1)(a)); or

(b) a requirement for the payee to give a notice to the payer or a specified person within the same 5-day period (s.110A(1)(b)).

The 5-day period is the statutory compliance requirement imposed on the construction contract by s.110A(1). It is not a default that parties may freely adjust. If a contract does not include a compliant payment notice provision, the Scheme for Construction Contracts applies as the statutory fallback (s.110A(5)).

*Which route applies:* Where the contract requires the payer or a specified person to give the notice (s.110A(1)(a)), the primary obligation rests on the payer side. Many standard-form contracts (including NEC and JCT variants) instead require the contractor or payee to give their own notice (s.110A(1)(b)); a contractor's interim payment application given under such a contract functions as the operative payment notice. Where this is the case, it results from the contractual arrangement, not from s.110A(1)(a).

*Fallback where payer fails to give notice (s.110B):* Where the contract requires the payer or a specified person to give a notice under s.110A(1)(a) but no notice is given, the payee has a right under s.110B to give their own notice at any time after the deadline for the payer's notice. When the payee uses this right, the final date for payment is postponed by the same number of days that the payee's notice is given after the required date for the payer's notice.

**Pay less notice — s.111:** A paying party may not withhold payment after the final date for payment unless it has given an effective pay less notice specifying: (i) the sum the payer considers to be due, and (ii) the basis on which that sum is calculated. The notice must be served no later than the prescribed period before the final date for payment (set by the contract or, absent contractual provision, the Scheme).

**Right to suspend — s.112:** A party has the right to suspend performance where: (1) a sum due is not paid in full by the final date for payment; (2) no effective pay less notice has been given; and (3) the suspending party has given at least 7 days written notice of intention to suspend, stating the grounds. The right does not arise automatically on non-payment — the 7-day notice is a precondition.

### Important qualifications

- Professional services appointments (surveying, architecture) ARE within the Act's scope when they relate to construction operations (s.104(2)); a pure professional consultancy unrelated to active construction operations may be different
- The 5-day payment notice period is a statutory compliance requirement on the construction contract (s.110A(1)), not a default that parties may freely vary — a contract that fails to provide a compliant payment notice mechanism within 5 days does not comply with s.110A and the Scheme applies as the statutory fallback (s.110A(5))
- The final date for payment and the pay less notice deadline are set by the contract or, if the contract is silent or non-compliant, by the Scheme — these are genuinely contract-specific and should not be quoted as universal figures
- The Scheme for Construction Contracts sets fallback provisions; its specific default periods were not separately verified in this review — do not quote Scheme-specific figures as authoritative without checking the current Scheme regulations
- Scotland has some distinct provisions; candidates on Scottish projects should be directed to check the Scottish position

### Common errors to avoid

- Stating a £1 million (or any other value) minimum contract threshold — no such threshold exists
- Stating that surveying and architectural appointments are categorically outside the Act — they are included under s.104(2)
- Describing the right to stage payment as unqualified — note the 45-day duration exception under s.109
- Omitting the pay less notice when explaining the payment mechanism — name the statutory instrument; "deductions" alone is insufficient
- Describing the right to suspend as arising automatically on non-payment — the 7-day written notice requirement under s.112(2) must be met first
- Treating the 5-day payment notice period as a Scheme default that parties may freely override — it is the statutory requirement placed on the construction contract by s.110A(1); the Scheme applies only if the contract does not include a compliant mechanism
- Conflating the payment notice period (5 days after the payment due date, set by s.110A(1)) with the final date for payment or the pay less notice deadline — these are separate and are set by the contract or the Scheme independently
- Stating that s.110A only allows the payer to give the payment notice — the contract may alternatively require the payee to give their own notice (s.110A(1)(b))
- Conflating the residential occupier exclusion with a monetary exclusion

### Source gaps

- Scheme for Construction Contracts (England and Wales) Regulations 1998 as amended — specific default periods not verified in this review
- Scotland-specific provisions not examined

| Applicable version | SI 2012/632 as in force |
| Effective date | 6 April 2012 |
| Verification date | 24 September 2026 |
| Source consulted | legislation.gov.uk — CAR 2012 Regulations 4 and 9; HSE guidance (hse.gov.uk search results) |
| Source review status | AUTHORITATIVE_VERIFIED (Regulations 4 and 9 confirmed from legislation.gov.uk) |

### Verified professional requirements

**Regulation 4 — Duty to manage asbestos in non-domestic premises:**

The dutyholder must:
- Carry out a suitable and sufficient assessment of whether asbestos is or is liable to be present in the premises
- Prepare a written plan identifying the parts of the premises concerned and the risk management measures
- Ensure the condition of any asbestos or presumed asbestos is monitored
- Ensure that information about the location and condition of asbestos is provided to every person likely to disturb it and to emergency services

Regulation 4 does NOT require notification to the HSE upon discovery or identification of asbestos.

**Regulation 9 — Three categories of asbestos work:**

There are three categories under CAR 2012 with distinct requirements:

1. **Licensed asbestos work** — requires an HSE licence; at least 14 days written notice to the HSE before work starts (or a shorter period agreed with HSE under Reg 9(2)); also requires health surveillance and air monitoring.

2. **Notifiable non-licensed work (NNLW)** — does not require a licence but notification to the HSE is required before work commences (Reg 9(3)); no minimum number of days is prescribed — the requirement is notification before the work begins; also requires health surveillance, air monitoring, and written records of exposure.

3. **Non-notifiable non-licensed work** — requires neither an HSE licence nor HSE notification. Lower-risk, short-duration work with asbestos-containing materials below the NNLW thresholds falls into this category.

The trigger for notification is always the WORK ACTIVITY, not the identification or discovery of asbestos. Not all asbestos work is licensed or notifiable.

**Dutyholder and managing agent:** The Regulation 4 duty to manage rests with the dutyholder — the owner of the premises, or the person who by virtue of a contract or tenancy has an obligation of any extent in relation to the maintenance or repair of the non-domestic premises. Where no such person exists, the person in control of the premises is the dutyholder.

A managing agent acts on behalf of their client (typically the building owner) and co-ordinates management of the asbestos on the dutyholder's behalf. A managing agent is not automatically the statutory dutyholder in their own right. Whether a managing agent has taken on contractual maintenance or repair obligations that make them a dutyholder under Regulation 4 is fact-specific and depends on the terms of the managing agent agreement.

### Important qualifications

- Whether a managing agent who has contractual control over a building could themselves be a dutyholder under Regulation 4 depends on the specific contractual arrangement — this is fact-specific and requires professional advice in individual cases
- There are three categories of asbestos work under CAR 2012: licensed work; notifiable non-licensed work (NNLW); and non-notifiable non-licensed work — the obligations differ for each category; licensed work requires both notification and a licence from HSE
- Air monitoring and health surveillance requirements also vary by category

### Common errors to avoid

- Stating that a managing agent must notify the HSE simply upon becoming aware of asbestos — not a regulatory requirement
- Addressing a managing agent as the statutory dutyholder without qualification — the duty rests with the owner or person with contractual maintenance/repair obligations; a managing agent acts on their behalf
- Conflating the duty to manage (Regulation 4, no notification required) with the notification requirements for asbestos work (Regulation 9)
- Stating that all work that disturbs asbestos requires HSE notification — non-notifiable non-licensed work requires neither a licence nor notification
- Implying that all asbestos must be removed — the duty is to manage, which typically means managing it safely in place if in good condition
- Stating that the managing agent assumes the dutyholder's legal obligations in their own right

### Related CDM 2015 correction (not in CAR 2012, but relevant to same competency)

Pre-construction information and the health and safety file are separate CDM 2015 documents:
- Pre-construction information: compiled before construction; provided to designers and contractors by the principal designer
- Health and safety file: record of the completed project; compiled by the principal designer; handed to the client on project completion for future reference

Source: Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015
